About

The mind behind
the purchase

I study how belonging gets built — through objects, environments, systems, and history — and what happens to people who operate outside those systems. Most readings of wealth are surface-level. People project what they would do with money onto people operating at a completely different level, then call that a moral position. I'm more interested in understanding how the system actually works.

"The question isn't whether someone is good or bad for having wealth. The question is whether you understand the system well enough to have an opinion worth listening to."

My work sits at the intersection of behavioral science, luxury economics, and cultural history. The case studies apply academic research to real brands and real consumer moments. The cultural work traces how power, access, and identity have always operated — long before luxury brands existed to package it. The frameworks are original attempts to systematize what I keep observing.

Based in Canada. Eyes on London, Dubai, and everywhere the interesting conversations about wealth and desire are happening.
